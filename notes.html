Behind the build.

Notes for a fictional dog-daycare software concept, including original thinking, an AI-assisted product plan and the actual alpha tests carried out on the published prototype.

Assigned case H

Waggy Tails Dog Daycare

Case business: Waggy Tails Dog Daycare, Lucan, County Dublin. Fictional owner: Aisling Nolan. The brief specifies 40 spaces daily, six staff, overbookings, paper vaccination records and frequent pet-owner update requests.

Agreed alpha: Bookings, Today and Pupdate, designed for quick one-tap use by staff. Sample data only: the app is a browser-based prototype, not a live pet-care service.

Task 1 · original

Hunch (before AI)

Retained exactly as originally written in the working discussion; spelling and phrasing intentionally unchanged.

I think customers who would understand the advantages of tehcnological imlementations like these would pay - especially if the owner communicates that weve been missing vaccination dates, keeping guys waiting because were overbooked etc

Later refinement (AI-assisted)

The paying customer is more likely to be the daycare owner, rather than the pet owner. The refined hypothesis is that independently run, less digitised service businesses may pay to reduce administration, avoid booking errors and improve the customer experience. This refinement does not replace the original hunch.

Task 1 · niche

Audience and problem

Specific niche: independent Irish dog daycare centres, especially single-site operators with approximately 15–40 places per day and small teams.

BuyerOwner or manager of the dog daycare, responsible for spending and operations.
Everyday usersDaycare staff managing spaces, vaccination checks and brief activity updates. Pet owners use the Pupdate preview.

Pain in the niche's words

Both lines below are invented illustrations, not actual customer research or testimonials.

“We've promised another dog a place, but Friday is already full.”

“I'm trying to look after the dogs, not answer messages all afternoon.”

Technology type: pet-care booking and daily-activity management system, with a restricted owner-facing status preview.

Current ways of working (case-based): bookings through WhatsApp, Instagram and texts; paper vaccination records; individual owner messages for updates. This describes the fictional assigned case, not a verified claim about every Irish daycare.

Task 1 · market scan

Two existing alternatives

1. Gingr — dog daycare software. Its official site describes online booking, check-in, capacity controls, vaccination indicators, messaging and pet report cards.

2. PetExec — daycare management. Its official site describes daycare scheduling, vaccine dates/alerts and tools for handling reservations.

Official provider pages checked 19 October 2026. These products cover parts of the same operational problem; this is not a claim that PawPilot is uniquely capable of those features.

Task 1 · lean PRD

Five-line product requirements document

  1. Hypothesis: A clear offer for simple booking, vaccination and one-tap pet updates will attract interest from independent Irish dog daycare owners.
  2. Audience: Owner-managers of independent, single-site Irish dog daycares with small teams.
  3. One call to action: “Join early access” — explicitly a demo interaction, not a live waiting list.
  4. Measure success: In a future live launch, track genuinely consented early-access enquiries versus unique landing-page visits. The current form collects no data and therefore produces no real conversion metric.
  5. Out of scope: Payment processing, live customer registration, secure owner authentication, actual messaging, vaccination-document verification and multi-business cloud synchronisation.
Task 2 · actual alpha test

What I tested

I published the Waggy Tails browser demo and tested its controls using fictional sample data.

  • Made bookings and refreshed the page — bookings remained saved.
  • Tried booking a dog with expired vaccination — booking was blocked.
  • Added a one-tap activity note in Today and refreshed — the note remained saved.
  • Opened Pupdate — only the selected dog's updates appeared in that view.
  • Tried booking on a 40/40 day — the booking was blocked and an alternative day suggested.
  • Checked Today's warnings — amber for vaccinations approaching expiry and red for expired vaccination, with check-in prevented.

What went wrong or confused me

The public link was not accessible when I first tried to publish the alpha. I had not enabled deployment from the main branch in GitHub Pages settings.

What I changed

I changed the Pages publishing source to Deploy from a branch → main → /(root), saved the setting and waited for deployment. I then opened the published link successfully.

This is a genuine publishing/configuration fix, not a claim that a code bug was fixed. The functional outcomes above are the tests reported during this build; cross-device performance and production security are not certified.

Task 3 · claim check

What the sales page can honestly promise

Demonstrated in the alpha: sample-data day capacity, vaccination warnings and booking restrictions, one-tap activity entries, an owner-view preview, and local browser persistence.

Not yet delivered: genuine business accounts, private owner authentication, live customer notifications, multi-device shared data, payment collection and a real waiting-list service.

The landing-page form displays a thank-you message only; no information is sent or saved. The proposed price is an assumption. The screenshots, illustrative examples and fictional case are not presented as evidence of real commercial adoption.
